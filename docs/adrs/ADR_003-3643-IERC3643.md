## Context 

The ERC-3643 standard defines a set of interfaces to support regulatory-compliant token operations. These interfaces are published under the ERC-3643 GitHub repository [https://github.com/ERC-3643/ERCs/blob/master/assets/erc-3643/interfaces] and include components for identity, compliance, recovery, freezing, and more.

Among them, IERC3643 is the main interface that directly extends IERC20, and serves as the entry point for most of the standard’s regulatory and operational features, such as:

- Identity and compliance integration
- Full and partial token freezing
- Forced transfers and recovery
- Contract pausing
- Batch operations
- Extended token metadata

However, IERC3643 is defined as a monolithic interface, which is incompatible with our modular design principles based on the Diamond pattern (EIP-2535). In our implementation, each functional group must be isolated into its own module composed of:

- An internal contract that encapsulates business logic and manages storage
- An external contract that exposes public methods and delegates to the internal logic
- A facet that connects the external contract to the Diamond via delegatecall

This structure ensures:

- Separation of concerns: Each module handles a specific domain (e.g., freezing, recovery, batch).
- Avoidance of selector clashes: Prevents overlapping function selectors in the Diamond.
- Code reuse: Shared logic is centralized in internal contracts like ERC20InternalCommon.
- Upgradeability and maintainability: Modules can be independently updated or replaced.

Additionally, a key challenge in this modularization process is determining whether existing interfaces —such as IERC20Isbe or IPause— can be reused within the context of IERC3643. While method signatures may match, the ERC-3643 standard introduces stricter semantics, including:

- Role-based access control (e.g., onlyAgent)
- Specific event structures and expectations

This raises the need to evaluate interface reuse on a case-by-case basis to ensure compliance without sacrificing modularity or reusability.

## Decision

### 1. Segregation of IERC3643 into functional modules

To align with our Diamond architecture, the monolithic IERC3643 interface will be split into independent functional modules, each implemented through:

- A dedicated interface (e.g., IERC20Freeze, IERC20Recovery, etc.)
- An internal contract that encapsulates the business logic and manages storage
- An external contract that exposes public methods and delegates to the internal logic
- A facet that connects the external contract to the Diamond via delegatecall

When functionality is shared across multiple modules—such as token metadata, pause state, or access control—logic is centralized in abstract internal contracts (e.g., ERC20InternalCommon, PauseInternalCommon)

### 2. Reuse of existing interfaces under stricter semantics

To ensure compliance with ERC-3643 while maximizing modularity and reuse, the following strategy has been adopted:

- Reuse existing interfaces (e.g., IPause, IERC20Isbe) when method signatures, event definitions, and semantics are fully aligned with ERC-3643.
- Extend or redefine interfaces when ERC-3643 introduces stricter requirements—such as additional access control (onlyAgent), event emission, or behavioral constraints. For example:
    - IERC20Isbe3643 extends IERC20Isbe to include setName and setSymbol with onlyOwner access and event emission.
    - ISBEPause3643 reuses IPause but enforces TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE at the external contract level.

This approach ensures that the Diamond architecture remains compliant with ERC-3643 while avoiding unnecessary duplication of interfaces and maximizing reuse of existing components.

### 3. Functional mapping of IERC3643 methods (FALTARA AÑADIR LOS EVENTOS, QUE NO SE PQ NO SE AÑADIERON DESDE EL PRINCIPIO)

The implementation of the IERC3643 interface within the Diamond architecture requires a precise mapping of each method to its corresponding module. Each method is delegated through a layered structure composed of:

- A dedicated interface that defines the method signature and expected behavior
- An internal contract that encapsulates the business logic and manages storage
- An external contract that implements the interface and delegates to the internal logic
- A facet that connects the external contract to the Diamond via delegatecall

While some existing modules (e.g., ERC20Capped, ERC20Burnable) already provide partial implementations, their external contracts do not fully comply with the access control and semantics required by IERC3643. For example, the mint method in ERC20Capped uses onlyRole(_MINTER_ROLE), whereas IERC3643 expects stricter agent-based control.

- To ensure full compliance with the standard, new external contracts will be created and paired with corresponding facets. These will implement the required interfaces and delegate to existing internal logic where applicable.
- For modules not yet implemented, new internal contracts will be created and paired with new external contracts and facets.

The IERC20Isbe3643 interface extends the existing IERC20Isbe interface to include additional methods required by the ERC-3643 standard, specifically:

- setName(string)
- setSymbol(string)

These methods are not present in the original IERC20Isbe and introduce stricter semantics:

- Access control: Restricted to the contract owner (onlyOwner)
- Event emission: Emit the UpdatedTokenInformation event defined in IERC3643

Although the methods name(), symbol(), and decimals() are inherited unchanged from IERC20Isbe, they are exposed through IERC20Isbe3643 to maintain consistency and traceability within the modular ERC-3643 architecture. This ensures that all token metadata operations—both read and write—are grouped under a single functional module.

Note: The methods setName and setSymbol are not added to the base ERC20 contract, as their semantics are specific to the ERC-3643 regulatory context. Including them in a generic ERC20 implementation would violate the principle of separation of concerns and could lead to unintended behavior in non-regulated token deployments.

In this architecture, the external contract ERC203643 implements the IERC20Isbe3643 interface and:

- Applies access control (onlyOwner) and emits the UpdatedTokenInformation event for setName and setSymbol
- Delegates all logic to internal methods defined in ERC20Internal (via ERC20InternalCommon), including:
    - _name(), _symbol(), _decimals() for read operations
    - _setName(string), _setSymbol(string) for write operations

This separation ensures that the interface remains semantically strict, while the internal contract encapsulates the actual state changes and logic, in line with the Diamond architecture principles.

The ERC-3643 specification defines the pause(), unpause(), and paused() methods with strict regulatory semantics, as documented in IERC3643.sol. These methods are intended to restrict token transfers when the contract is paused, and can only be invoked by wallets designated as agents of the token issuer.

In the ISBE architecture, the Pause module was originally designed as a general-purpose mechanism to restrict contract operations. It uses modifiers like whenNotPaused and whenPaused, which can be applied to any function—including token transfers—making it compatible with the intent of ERC-3643 even if it was not originally tailored for that standard.

To enforce the specific access control semantics required by ERC-3643, a dedicated external contract ISBEPause3643 is introduced. This contract:

- Overrides the _checkPauserRoles() and _getAuthorityLevel() methods to enforce a dedicated TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE, representing the authorized agent of the token issuer.
- Reuses all internal logic from the existing Pause module, including state management and event emission.
- Ensures that only authorized agents can pause or unpause the contract, as required by the standard.

To make this semantic constraint explicit and prevent misuse or misinterpretation by other developers, a dedicated interface IPause3643 is introduced. This interface:

- Extends the existing IPause interface without modifying method signatures.
- Documents the stricter access control requirements and event expectations imposed by ERC-3643.
- Serves as a clear contract for developers implementing regulated pause functionality under the ERC-3643 standard.

This approach ensures semantic clarity, regulatory compliance, and architectural consistency within the modular ISBE implementation.

### Module: ERC20 Mint, Burn and Transfer

| Method                            | Interface              | Internal             | External               | Facet                       | Comment                                                                 |
|-----------------------------------|------------------------|-----------------------|-------------------------|------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `mint(address,uint256)`          | `IERC20MintBurn3643`   | `ERC20InternalCommon` | `ERC20MintBurn3643`     | `ERC20MintBurn3643Facet`     | Requiere `TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE`. Solo a direcciones verificadas.           |
| `burn(address,uint256)`          | `IERC20MintBurn3643`   | `ERC20InternalCommon` | `ERC20MintBurn3643`     | `ERC20MintBurn3643Facet`     | Requiere `TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE`. Puede desbloquear tokens congelados.      |
| `forcedTransfer(address,address,uint256)` | `IERC20MintBurn3643` | `ERC20InternalCommon` | `ERC20MintBurn3643`     | `ERC20MintBurn3643Facet`     | Requiere `TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE`. Reduce tokens congelados si es necesario. |



The ERC-3643 specification defines mint, burn, and forcedTransfer as privileged operations that must be executed by authorized agents of the token issuer. These methods are semantically stricter than their counterparts in generic ERC-20 extensions like ERC20Capped, ERC20Burnable, or ERC20Controller, which typically rely on roles like _MINTER_ROLE or _CONTROLLER_ROLE.

- Minting must be restricted to verified addresses and executed only by agents.
- Burning must handle frozen balances, unlocking tokens if necessary to complete the operation.
- Forced transfers must allow moving tokens even when the sender’s free balance is insufficient, by unlocking frozen tokens if needed.

To ensure compliance, a new external contract ERC20MintBurn3643 is introduced. This contract:

- Implements the IERC20MintBurn3643 interface, which defines ERC-3643-compliant versions of mint, burn, and forcedTransfer.
- Applies TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE checks to all methods.
- Validates recipient addresses for minting.
- Handles frozen balances during burning and forced transfers.
- Emits Transfer and TokensUnfrozen events as required.
- Delegates logic to ERC20InternalCommon, preserving reuse and consistency.


This separation ensures that the ERC-3643 regulatory requirements are met without modifying the generic ERC-20 modules, maintaining modularity and clarity in the Diamond architecture.

The ERC20 Freeze module implements both full and partial freezing mechanisms as defined in the ERC-3643 standard. These operations are critical for regulatory compliance and must be executed exclusively by authorized agents.

### Module: ERC20 Recovery

| Method                          | Interface               | Internal               | External              | Facet                   | Comment                                                                 |
|----------------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `recoveryAddress(address,address,address)` | `IERC20Recovery3643` | `ERC20RecoveryInternal` | `ERC20Recovery3643`    | `ERC20RecoveryFacet`     | Requires `TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE`. Emits `RecoverySuccess`, `TokensUnfrozen`, and `Transfer`. |

The ERC20 Recovery module implements the recoveryAddress function as defined in the ERC-3643 standard. This function allows an authorized agent to recover tokens from a lost wallet to a new one, based on the investor’s on-chain identity.

To comply with the standard:

- A dedicated interface IERC20Recovery3643 is introduced, defining the recoveryAddress method.
- The method can only be executed by an address with the TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE, ensuring that only authorized agents can initiate a recovery.
- The function emits multiple events upon success:
    - RecoverySuccess: Indicates a successful recovery operation.
    - TokensUnfrozen: Emitted if frozen tokens had to be unlocked during the process.
    - Transfer: Emitted to reflect the token movement from the lost to the new wallet.
- The internal contract ERC20RecoveryInternal encapsulates the logic for:
    - Validating the recovery request.
    - Unlocking frozen tokens if necessary.
    - Transferring the balance.
- The external contract ERC20Recovery3643 applies access control and delegates the logic to the internal contract.
- The facet ERC20RecoveryFacet connects the module to the Diamond architecture.

This design ensures that recovery operations are secure, auditable, and compliant with ERC-3643, while maintaining modularity and separation of concerns.

The Regulatory Infrastructure module provides the foundational configuration for ERC-3643-compliant tokens, including identity, compliance, and metadata linkage. These elements are essential for ensuring that the token operates within a regulated framework.

To meet the standard’s requirements:

- A dedicated interface IERCRegulatoryInfra3643 is introduced, grouping all infrastructure-related methods.
- The following setter methods are restricted to the contract owner (onlyOwner):
    - setOnchainID(address): Updates the token’s on-chain identity and emits UpdatedTokenInformation.
    - setIdentityRegistry(address): Links the token to an identity registry and emits IdentityRegistryAdded.
    - setCompliance(address): Links the token to a compliance contract, emits ComplianceAdded, and calls bindToken on the compliance contract.
- Getter methods (onchainID, version, identityRegistry, compliance) provide read-only access to the current configuration.
- The internal contract RegulatoryInfra3643Internal encapsulates all logic and state management.
- The external contract RegulatoryInfra3643 applies access control and delegates logic to the internal contract.
- The facet RegulatoryInfra3643Facet connects the module to the Diamond architecture.

This module ensures that the token’s regulatory context is clearly defined, auditable, and updatable in a secure and modular way.

The Batch Operations module provides high-efficiency methods for executing multiple token operations in a single transaction, as defined by the ERC-3643 standard. These include batch transfers, minting, burning, freezing, and forced transfers.

To ensure compliance and performance:

- A dedicated interface IERC20BatchOps3643 is introduced, grouping all batch-related methods.
- All write operations are restricted to agents with the TOKEN_AGENT_ROLE, except for batchTransfer, which can be executed by any non-frozen sender.
- Each method emits multiple events corresponding to the number of operations performed:
    - Transfer events for transfers, minting, burning, and forced transfers.
    - TokensUnfrozen for forced transfers when frozen tokens are unlocked.
    - AddressFrozen, TokensFrozen, and TokensUnfrozen for freeze-related operations.
- The internal contract ERC20BatchInternal encapsulates the logic for validating inputs, managing state, and emitting events.
- The external contract ERC20BatchOps3643 applies access control and delegates logic to the internal contract.
- The facet ERC20BatchFacet connects the module to the Diamond architecture.

Note: Due to potential gas limitations, these methods should be used with caution when processing large arrays. The documentation explicitly warns about the risk of out-of-gas errors.


# Duda para el equipo: ¿Dónde deben definirse los modificadores y eventos en nuestra arquitectura Diamond?
En nuestra arquitectura basada en el patrón Diamond, estamos separando la lógica en contratos internos y externos. Sin embargo, hay ambigüedad sobre dónde deben residir los modificadores de acceso y la emisión de eventos, ya que observamos enfoques distintos en distintos módulos.

Por ejemplo, en el módulo ERC20Capped:

- El contrato externo (ERC20Capped) aplica los modificadores (onlyRole, whenNotPaused, checkCap, etc.) y emite eventos como CapSet.
- El contrato interno (ERC20CappedInternal) encapsula únicamente la lógica de almacenamiento y validación, sin emitir eventos ni aplicar modificadores.

Este enfoque parece alineado con una separación estricta de responsabilidades:

- Contratos externos: control de acceso, validaciones de contexto, emisión de eventos.
- Contratos internos: lógica pura y acceso a almacenamiento.

Sin embargo, en otros módulos como ERC20Internal, los eventos (Transfer, Approval) y modificadores (addressIsNotZero) se aplican directamente en el contrato interno, por estar estrechamente ligados al flujo de ejecución y a los hooks (_beforeTokenTransfer, _afterTokenTransfer).

Esto plantea una duda importante de diseño:

- ¿Queremos establecer una regla general sobre dónde deben ir los modificadores y eventos?

Opciones posibles:

- Eventos y modificadores siempre en el contrato externo, manteniendo los internos como lógica pura.
- Eventos ligados a cambios de estado (como Transfer) en el contrato interno, y eventos/modificadores administrativos en el externo.
- Evaluar caso por caso, sin una regla estricta, pero documentando claramente la decisión en cada módulo.

Esta decisión afecta directamente a la reutilización de contratos internos como ERC20Internal desde múltiples contratos externos, y a la claridad de responsabilidades en la arquitectura.
